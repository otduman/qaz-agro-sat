# HLS test: results

9 November 2026, branch `hls-test`. Follows the Landsat test (`2026-11-08-landsat-test.md`, brought into this
branch).

## Why

The Landsat test found Landsat (Collection 2, level 2) reading our fields with NDVI 0.03 to 0.04 higher and SWIR about
0.02 lower than our Sentinel-2 series. The owner chose NASA's published adjustment, checked against our pairs. NASA's
bandpass coefficients ([HLS bandpass adjustment](https://hls.gsfc.nasa.gov/algorithms/bandpass-adjustment)) turned out
to be tiny: SWIR 1.6 slope 0.9987 and offset -0.0011 for Sentinel-2A. They cannot explain a 0.02 difference. Our
difference must come mostly from the two archives' different atmospheric corrections (and viewing angles, and our use
of the wide near-infrared band B08). NASA's Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2 products (HLS v2.0) process both satellites
the same way, at 30 m, and Planetary Computer serves them for our tile (T41UMV) with no account. So, with the owner's
go-ahead, this test reads every field on HLS as the Landsat test read Landsat.

## How

`HlsCheck` (experiment module): collections `hls2-s30` and `hls2-l30`, bands red, narrow near-infrared (B8A on
Sentinel-2, B05 on Landsat), SWIR 1.6 and 2.2, blue, green; scale 0.0001, nodata -9999. Clear means HLS's Fmask
shows no cloud, cloud-adjacent, shadow or snow bit, plus our snow test. Fields are the current (field-lines) maps;
interiors, the 60% clear share and medians as in step 3. To read the HLS bands, the COG reader now reads signed
16-bit files (test first). About 6 minutes per season. Readings in `data/hls-<year>/hls-series-<year>.csv`.

## What this means

- **Inside HLS, the two satellites agree well in SWIR** (within 0.006), the band the cut signal relies on. NDVI still
  differs by about 0.02.
- **HLS is not our Sentinel-2.** HLS Sentinel-2 reads NDVI 0.04 to 0.07 higher than our series, and the gap changes
  from season to season. Moving the curves to HLS would change every level step 3 measured and drop to 30 m.
- **HLS fills fewer gaps than Collection 2 Landsat.** Some Landsat dates are missing from HLS (18 and 19 August 2025,
  on which Collection 2 had 294 fields clear), and HLS's cloud mask, which also flags pixels next to cloud, keeps
  fewer fields clear (10 August 2025: 47 fields against 101). With HLS Landsat, the worst 2025 gap stays at 28 days.
- **HLS Landsat and Collection 2 Landsat differ from our series by about the same amounts** (NDVI +0.03, SWIR -0.02),
  steady across seasons. So the difference is between the two archives' processing, and a fitted adjustment can
  remove it.

So HLS does not solve it. The better route looks like Collection 2 Landsat (most clear dates) with our own
per-band adjustment, fitted on 2025's same-day pairs only and then checked on 2024 and 2023. That is for the owner to
decide.
