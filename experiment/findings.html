The answer so far

Partly. When a field is cut, it usually turns paler in shortwave infrared (SWIR), the satellite band that sees dry straw and soil best, while uncut fields stay flat. But a simple rule built on that jump is not good enough yet. On data it had never seen, it dated the cut to within one satellite photo, about a week, for only 17 of 29 fields.

NDVI, the residue index NDTI and radar barely moved at the cut. NDVI drops weeks earlier, when the crop ripens. Radar dropped slightly at the cut once each orbit was compared only with itself, but uncut fields moved almost as much, and there are no radar images of this area for 2023 or 2024.

A fixed rule, tested on a fresh season, did worse

After rounds 1 to 3 I wrote rule v2 to fix the misses seen so far. It ignores dates after 20 October, drops one-date spikes, and keeps the last standing-crop date. It was committed before any 2023 data was downloaded. On 2023 it dated 5 of 13 cut fields within one photo, against 7 of 13 for the original rule. Keeping the standing-crop date made it pick up ripening in August instead of the cut. So v2 is rejected, and its better score on rounds 1 to 3 was overfitting. What the misses taught

  • Some cuts make a field darker, not paler. In 2023, a pale ripe field turned darker after the cut, with bright straw rows on dark stubble. A rule that only looks for brightening misses these.
  • Late crops never look ripe to the rule. Three 2023 fields never dropped below NDVI 0.4, so the rule never started watching them.
  • Fields cut right after ripening. Fields cut before any clear photo showed them ripe are invisible to the rule.
  • Snow, wet ground and haze. A near-white snow day on 27 October 2024 and thin haze on a few dates got past the cloud mask and made fake jumps.
  • Swathing. Where the crop is first cut into rows and threshed later, brightness rose slowly over weeks instead of jumping.
  • Cloud gaps. In 2025, 9 of the 20 blind-test crop fields were cut during a cloudy August and early September, with no clear photo between ripe and cut.
  • Boxes drawn on a map. 7 of the 35 boxes straddled a field boundary in at least one year, because fields are re-split between seasons. Real field boundaries, drawn fresh each season, are needed.
  • What the eye uses is texture. When I read the photos, the clearest sign of a cut was parallel lines appearing: combine tracks and swath rows. Brightness came second. A texture measure from the 10 m bands is the most promising signal to try next.

What this does not prove yet

  • The cut dates are my reading of the photos, not ground truth. Several are only moderate calls, and the notes say which. Nobody on the ground has confirmed a single date. Because my reading leans on texture, a texture-based rule would need ground truth to be judged fairly.
  • One small area. All boxes are in one 20 × 20 km area of Kostanay oblast, over three seasons.
  • Few uncut controls in 2023 and 2024. Crop rotation turned most controls into crop fields, so the false-alarm test rests on 12 boxes in all.
