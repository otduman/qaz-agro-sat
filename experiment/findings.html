The answer so far

Partly yes. When a field is cut, it turns paler in shortwave infrared (SWIR), the satellite band that sees dry straw and soil best. That jump showed up in every round, while uncut fields stayed flat. A simple rule built on it dates the cut to within one satellite photo for 16 of 22 fields, which is about a week. That isn't yet reliable enough for a client.

NDVI, the residue index NDTI and radar barely moved at the cut. NDVI drops weeks earlier, when the crop ripens. Radar dropped slightly at the cut once each orbit was compared only with itself, but uncut fields moved almost as much.

What the misses taught

  • Fields cut right after ripening. The rule only watches a field once it looks ripe, with NDVI below 0.4. Two 2024 fields were cut in the second half of August, before any clear photo showed them ripe, so the rule never saw them standing.
  • Snow and late-season wet ground. On 27 October 2024 one field turned almost white, with NDVI near zero, which looks like snow. It got past the cloud mask and made a fake jump. Wet ground after the cut pulled the brightness back down on another field. The rule needs to stop before the first snow and to treat rain days with care.
  • Haze the cloud mask misses. Thin haze on a few dates passed as clear and made one-date spikes.
  • Swathing. Where the crop is first cut into rows and threshed later, brightness rose slowly over weeks instead of jumping.
  • August cloud. In 2025, 9 of the 20 blind-test crop fields were cut during a cloudy August and early September, with no clear photo between ripe and cut. No optical method can date those. Radar sees through cloud, so it's still worth a better attempt.
  • Boxes drawn on a map. 7 of the 35 boxes straddled a field boundary in at least one year, including 4 picked by the fixed rule, because fields are re-split between seasons. Real field boundaries, drawn fresh each season, are needed before any per-field numbers mean much.

What this does not prove yet

  • The cut dates are my reading of the photos, not ground truth. Several are only moderate calls, and the notes say which. Nobody on the ground has confirmed a single date.
  • Round 1 tuned the rule. Rounds 2 and 3 were read blind with the rule frozen, so they are the fairer score: 10 of 16 within one photo.
  • One small area. All boxes are in one 20 × 20 km area of Kostanay oblast, over two seasons.
  • No radar for 2024. Planetary Computer has no 2024 Sentinel-1 images over this area, so round 3 is optical only.
